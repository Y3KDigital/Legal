**Document Date:** January 14, 2026  
**Status:** GENIUS Act signed into law July 18, 2025 | CLARITY Act under development

## Executive Summary

The United States has established the world's first comprehensive federal regulatory framework for stablecoins through two landmark pieces of legislation:

1. **GENIUS Act (S.1582)** - **SIGNED INTO LAW** on July 18, 2025
   - Full name: "Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act"
   - Provides regulatory framework for **payment stablecoins**
   - Establishes clear issuer requirements, reserve backing rules, and consumer protections

2. **CLARITY Act (H.R.4766)** - **Passed House, advancing in Senate**
   - Full name: "Clarity for Payment Stablecoins Act of 2023"
   - Complements GENIUS Act with additional regulatory clarity
   - Focuses on depository institution involvement and state-level regulation

3. **Keep Innovation in America Act (H.R.1414)** - Supporting legislation
   - Clarifies tax treatment and broker definitions for digital assets
   - Eliminates problematic cash reporting requirements

## Part 1: GENIUS Act - Now Federal Law

### 1.1 Overview

**Status:** Became federal law on July 18, 2025, signed by President Trump

**Purpose:** Establish first-of-its-kind federal regulatory framework for payment stablecoins to:
- Protect consumers
- Strengthen national security
- Support U.S. dollar dominance globally
- Enable faster, cheaper, and more accessible payments
- Provide regulatory certainty for innovators

**Key Achievement:** United States is now the first country with comprehensive federal stablecoin legislation.

### 1.2 Who Can Issue Stablecoins

Under the GENIUS Act, **only permitted issuers** can issue payment stablecoins for use by U.S. persons. Permitted issuers fall into three categories:

#### **Option 1: Subsidiary of Insured Depository Institution**
- Must be a subsidiary of a federally insured bank or credit union
- Regulated by federal banking regulators (OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC)
- Inherits regulatory oversight from parent institution
- **Advantages:** Established regulatory relationship, existing infrastructure, consumer trust
- **Requirements:** Bank Holding Company Act compliance, capital requirements

#### **Option 2: Federal-Qualified Nonbank Payment Stablecoin Issuer**
- Independent company regulated directly by federal regulators
- Must obtain federal qualification and ongoing supervision
- **Regulated by:** Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) or Federal Reserve
- **Advantages:** Can operate nationwide without bank charter, fintech-friendly
- **Requirements:** Robust compliance program, regular examinations, capital adequacy

#### **Option 3: State-Qualified Payment Stablecoin Issuer**
- Company licensed and supervised by state regulators
- Must meet state-specific requirements (varies by jurisdiction)
- **Regulated by:** State banking departments or financial services agencies
- **Advantages:** May have lower barriers to entry, state-specific expertise
- **Requirements:** State licensing, compliance with state money transmission laws, potential multi-state registration

### 1.3 Reserve Requirements (Critical Compliance Element)

Payment stablecoin issuers must maintain reserves backing the stablecoin **on a 1-to-1 basis**. This is the cornerstone of consumer protection.

#### **Eligible Reserve Assets:**

| Asset Class | Description | Requirements |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| **US Coins and Currency** | Physical cash | Must be held in custody |
| **US Treasury Securities** | Bills, notes, bonds | Short-term preferred for liquidity |
| **Central Bank Reserves** | Federal Reserve deposits | Direct account or via correspondent bank |
| **Repurchase Agreements (Repos)** | Secured by US Treasuries | Overnight or term, must be fully collateralized |
| **Other Approved Assets** | As determined by regulators | Subject to approval and risk weighting |

#### **Reserve Management Rules:**

1. **Segregation:** Reserves must be segregated from issuer's operational funds
2. **Bankruptcy Remote:** Structured to protect customer funds in issuer insolvency
3. **Daily Reconciliation:** Reserves must be reconciled daily with outstanding stablecoins
4. **Third-Party Custody:** Reserves held by qualified custodians (banks, trust companies)
5. **No Commingling:** Customer funds cannot be commingled with proprietary assets

### 1.4 Rehypothecation Restrictions

The GENIUS Act places strict limits on **rehypothecation** (reusing or pledging reserves as collateral):

- **Prohibited:** Lending reserve assets to third parties
- **Prohibited:** Using reserves as collateral for issuer's own borrowing
- **Permitted:** Limited repo transactions with approved counterparties (banks, primary dealers)
- **Reporting:** Any permitted rehypothecation must be disclosed in monthly reports

**Rationale:** Prevents systemic risk and ensures reserves are always available for redemptions.

### 1.5 Redemption Requirements

#### **Redemption Policy (Must be Publicly Disclosed):**
- **Timeline:** Redemption timeline (e.g., T+1, T+0, instant)
- **Process:** Step-by-step redemption procedures
- **Fees:** Any redemption fees or spreads
- **Limits:** Minimum/maximum redemption amounts
- **Restrictions:** Any holding periods or limitations

#### **Redemption Mechanics:**
1. Stablecoin holder submits redemption request
2. Issuer verifies holder identity (KYC/AML)
3. Stablecoin burned or destroyed
4. Equivalent fiat value released from reserves
5. Funds delivered to holder's designated account

#### **Consumer Protections:**
- **Par Redemption:** Redemption at $1.00 per stablecoin (or equivalent)
- **No Unfair Delays:** Issuers cannot unreasonably delay redemptions
- **Transparency:** Monthly public disclosure of reserve composition
- **Audit Requirements:** Annual third-party attestation of reserves

### 1.6 Disclosure and Reporting Obligations

#### **Monthly Public Disclosures (Published on Issuer Website):**

| Disclosure Item | Requirements |
|----------------|--------------|
| **Total Stablecoins Outstanding** | Exact number in circulation |
| **Total Reserve Value** | Market value of all reserve assets |
| **Reserve Composition** | Breakdown by asset type (cash, Treasuries, repos, etc.) |
| **Reserve Locations** | Custodians and account information |
| **Redemption Activity** | Volume and value of redemptions |
| **Attestation** | Link to most recent audit |

#### **Quarterly Reports to Regulators:**
- Financial statements (balance sheet, income statement, cash flow)
- Risk management assessments
- Compliance certifications
- Material changes to operations

#### **Annual Audits:**
- Independent CPA audit of reserves
- Attestation that reserves match outstanding stablecoins
- Confirmation of asset eligibility and custody arrangements
- Published within 90 days of fiscal year end

### 1.7 Securities Law Treatment (MAJOR WIN)

**Key Provision:** Payment stablecoins issued under the GENIUS Act framework **are NOT considered securities** under federal securities law.

**Implications:**
- ✅ No need to register with SEC
- ✅ Exempt from Securities Act of 1933 registration requirements
- ✅ Exempt from Securities Exchange Act of 1934 broker-dealer requirements
- ✅ Can be traded on non-securities exchanges
- ✅ Reduces regulatory burden and legal risk

**Caveat:** Stablecoins must be **payment stablecoins** as defined in the Act. This means:
- Redeemable on demand for fiat currency
- Backed 1-to-1 by eligible reserves
- Not marketed as an investment
- No expectation of profit from issuer's efforts

**Non-Payment Stablecoins:** Algorithmic stablecoins, yield-bearing stablecoins, or investment-linked stablecoins may still be securities subject to SEC jurisdiction.

### 1.8 Algorithmic Stablecoin Moratorium

**Two-Year Moratorium:** The GENIUS Act places a 2-year ban on new **endogenously collateralized stablecoins**.

**Definition:** Stablecoins that rely on the value of another digital asset created or maintained by the same originator to maintain their peg.

**Examples of Banned Stablecoins (for 2 years):**
- Algorithmic stablecoins (e.g., Terra/Luna model)
- Collateral is issuer's native token
- Relies on arbitrage or burning mechanisms

**Permitted After Moratorium:** Regulators may allow with additional safeguards after 2-year study period.

**Why:** Protects consumers following high-profile failures (Terra/UST collapse in 2022).

## Part 2: CLARITY Act - Advancing Through Congress

### 2.1 Overview

**Status:** Passed House on May 7, 2024, placed on Senate calendar

**Sponsor:** Rep. Patrick McHenry (R-NC-10)

**Purpose:** Provide additional clarity on payment stablecoin regulation with focus on:
- Role of insured depository institutions
- State-level regulatory frameworks
- Custody and safekeeping requirements
- Enforcement authority

### 2.2 Key Provisions (Complementary to GENIUS Act)

#### **Permitted Issuer Requirements (Similar but More Detailed):**

1. **Depository Institution Subsidiary:**
   - Must be subsidiary of FDIC-insured bank or credit union
   - Subject to supervision by federal banking agencies
   - Capital and liquidity requirements apply

2. **Federal Nonbank Issuer:**
   - Chartered by OCC or approved by Federal Reserve
   - Must meet capital requirements (risk-based capital ratio ≥ 10%)
   - Subject to examination and enforcement

3. **State-Qualified Issuer:**
   - Licensed by state banking or financial services department
   - Must comply with state money transmission laws
   - Limited to certain size thresholds (TBD by state)

#### **Reserve Requirements (Enhanced Specificity):**

- **Composition:** Minimum 90% in cash, US Treasuries, or central bank reserves
- **Maturity Limits:** Treasury securities ≤ 1 year maturity
- **Liquidity Buffer:** At least 10% in cash or overnight repos for redemptions
- **Diversification:** Limits on concentration with any single custodian (≤ 25%)

#### **Custody and Safekeeping:**

The CLARITY Act provides detailed requirements for custody services:

**Qualified Custodians:**
- Banks with ≥ $500M in assets
- Trust companies licensed to hold customer assets
- Registered broker-dealers with custody authority
- OCC-chartered trust companies

**Custodial Standards:**
- Segregated accounts for each issuer
- Daily reconciliation and reporting
- Insurance or bonding requirements
- Prohibition on self-custody (issuer cannot custody own reserves)

#### **Private Key Management:**

For digital asset custody (when reserves include digital assets):
- Multi-signature wallets (at least 3-of-5)
- Hardware security modules (HSMs) for key storage
- Geographic distribution of key holders
- Disaster recovery and business continuity plans

### 2.3 State Regulation Framework

**State Money Transmitter Licensing:**

States with existing money transmission laws can regulate stablecoin issuers if they meet federal standards:

| State | Status | Key Requirements |
|-------|--------|------------------|
| **New York** | BitLicense framework | Capital requirements, cybersecurity, consumer protection |
| **Wyoming** | Special Purpose Depository Institution (SPDI) | Bank-like charter for digital asset companies |
| **Texas** | Money Services Act | Bond requirements, net worth minimums |
| **Other States** | Uniform Money Services Act | Varies by state |

**Federal Preemption:** If state requirements conflict with federal law, federal law prevails.

### 2.4 Examination and Enforcement

**Supervisory Authority:**

- **Federal Issuers:** OCC, Federal Reserve, or FDIC (depending on structure)
  - Annual examinations (minimum)
  - Risk-based examination frequency (could be quarterly for high-risk issuers)
  - On-site and off-site monitoring

- **State Issuers:** State banking regulators
  - Coordination with federal regulators
  - Information sharing agreements
  - Joint examinations for interstate issuers

**Enforcement Powers:**

| Violation | Potential Penalties |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Reserve Deficiency** | Immediate suspension of issuance, civil penalties up to $1M/day |
| **Failure to Redeem** | Cease and desist order, receivership, restitution to holders |
| **Disclosure Violations** | Fines up to $500K per violation, injunctive relief |
| **Unauthorized Issuance** | Criminal penalties, forfeiture of assets, imprisonment |

### 3.1 Pre-Launch Requirements (6-12 Months)

| Path | Timeline | Cost | Complexity | Best For |

#### **Step 2: Corporate Structure**

**Recommended Entity Types:**
- **Delaware C Corporation** (most common for fundraising)
- **Wyoming LLC** (if crypto-native, SPDI path)

**Capital Requirements:**

| Federal Nonbank | $10M initial | $50M+ |

#### **Step 3: Reserve Infrastructure Setup**

**Banking Partners Needed:**
1. **Primary Custodian Bank** (Tier 1 bank for reserve accounts)
   - Examples: JP Morgan Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo
   - Requirements: Deposit account agreements, sweep accounts for excess reserves

2. **Backup Custodian** (Business continuity)
   - Geographic and institutional diversity
   - Mirror reserve structure

3. **Federal Reserve Access** (Optional but recommended)
   - Direct Federal Reserve account for reserves
   - Requires special application and approval
   - Provides highest safety and instant settlement

**Custodial Agreements Must Include:**
- Perfected security interest in reserves
- Issuer's right to inspect and audit
- Daily reporting obligations
- Disaster recovery provisions
- Termination and transition procedures

#### **Step 4: Technology Stack

**Core Systems Required:**

| System | Purpose | Vendors/Options |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| **Blockchain Platform** | Issue and manage stablecoins | Ethereum, Stellar, Algorand, Hedera, Solana |
| **Reserve Management System** | Track reserves, reconciliation | Custom build or TreasuryXpress, Reval |
| **KYC/AML Platform** | Customer verification | Chainalysis, Elliptic, Sumsub, Onfido |
| **Wallet Infrastructure** | Custody and transactions | Fireblocks, BitGo, Anchorage Digital |
| **Compliance Monitoring** | Transaction monitoring, sanctions screening | Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic |
| **Redemption Portal** | Customer-facing redemption interface | Custom or white-label (e.g., Prime Trust) |

**Smart Contract Requirements:**
- **Audited Code:** Minimum 2 independent security audits (e.g., Trail of Bits, OpenZeppelin)
- **Upgradeable:** Proxy pattern for bug fixes (but governance required)
- **Pause Function:** Emergency pause capability for security incidents
- **Blacklist Function:** Ability to freeze sanctioned addresses (OFAC compliance)
- **Mint/Burn Controls:** Multi-sig authorization for issuance and redemption

#### **Step 5: Compliance Program**

**Required Policies and Procedures:**

1. **Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) / Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Program**
   - Customer Identification Program (CIP)
   - Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
   - Suspicious Activity Reporting (SARs)
   - Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) for large transactions
   - Travel Rule compliance (FATF guidelines)

2. **Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) Sanctions Compliance**
   - Real-time sanctions screening
   - Blocked address lists (updated daily)
   - 50% rule for entity ownership
   - Reporting blocked transactions within 10 days

3. **Consumer Protection Policies**
   - Clear and conspicuous disclosures
   - Redemption policy and fee schedules
   - Privacy policy (GLBA compliance if applicable)
   - Error resolution procedures

4. **Information Security Program**
   - NIST Cybersecurity Framework compliance
   - Incident response plan
   - Data encryption (at rest and in transit)
   - Multi-factor authentication
   - Regular penetration testing

5. **Governance and Risk Management**
   - Board oversight and reporting
   - Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) appointment
   - Annual risk assessments
   - Internal audit function
   - Whistleblower hotline

### 3.2 Application and Approval Process

#### **Federal Nonbank Charter (OCC):**

**Application Package:**
1. **Business Plan** (50-100 pages)

   - Financial projections (5 years)
   - Organizational structure and management bios

   - Bylaws

4. **Compliance Manuals**
   - AML/BSA program

**Timeline:**
- **Pre-filing consultation:** 2-3 months (recommended)
- **Application submission:** 1 month to compile
- **OCC review:** 6-12 months (includes interviews, on-site visits)
- **Conditional approval:** Subject to pre-opening examination
- **Final approval:** After demonstrating operational readiness

**Fees:**
- Application fee: $50,000-$100,000 (varies by complexity)
- Supervision fees: Annual assessment based on asset size

#### **State Licensing (Example: New York BitLicense):**

**Application Requirements:**
1. Application fee: $5,000
2. Background checks: $1,500 per principal
3. Fingerprinting: $100 per individual
4. Bond or trust account: $500,000 minimum

**Documentation:**
- Business plan and financial projections
- AML/BSA program
- Cybersecurity policy
- Disaster recovery plan
- Consumer protection disclosures

**Timeline:**
- Application preparation: 2-3 months
- NYDFS review: 6-12 months
- Average approval time: 12-18 months

**Multi-State Registration:**
- If operating in multiple states, must obtain license in each state
- Consider Nationwide Multistate Licensing System (NMLS) for streamlined applications
- Some states have reciprocity agreements

### 3.5 Revenue Models

**How Stablecoin Issuers Make Money:**

1. **Interest on Reserves** (Primary revenue source)
   - Invest reserves in short-term US Treasuries (e.g., 1-month T-bills)
   - Current yield: ~4-5% annually (as of Jan 2026)
   - Example: $1B stablecoins = $40M-$50M annual revenue
   - Net margin: 70-80% after costs = $28M-$40M profit

2. **Redemption Fees** (Secondary)
   - Typical: 0.05%-0.1% per redemption
   - Example: $1B monthly redemptions × 0.1% = $1M/month = $12M/year

3. **Transaction Fees** (Minimal/optional)
   - Some issuers charge for premium services (instant settlements, large transactions)
   - Typically not charged to maintain adoption

4. **Custodial Services** (Adjacent business)
   - Provide wallet or custody services to institutional users
   - Fee: 0.5%-2% of assets under custody annually

**Profitability Timeline:**
- **Year 1:** Negative (setup costs)
- **Year 2:** Break-even or small profit (scale dependent)
- **Year 3+:** Profitable if $500M+ outstanding stablecoins
